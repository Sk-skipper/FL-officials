About and methodology

This directory gathers public information about elected officials in Florida into one searchable place. It is an independent project and is not run by any government agency.

Where the data comes from

Every official in the directory is collected automatically from an official government source, and every record links to the page it came from. Nothing is typed in by hand: when no official page can be collected, the office is listed under "Not yet covered" below instead of being filled in manually.

The sources, in order of authority:

  • Legislatures and Congress: the Florida Senate, the Florida House of Representatives, the U.S. Senate and the U.S. House of Representatives publish their own member lists.
  • Supervisors of Elections: the Florida Division of Elections lists all 67 Supervisors of Elections. The Broward and Miami-Dade Supervisors of Elections publish every office on their ballots: the Governor and Cabinet, the Florida Supreme Court and the District Courts of Appeal, circuit and county judges, the State Attorney and Public Defender, the sheriff, clerk, property appraiser and tax collector, the county commission and mayor, the school board, Miami-Dade's community councils and the South Dade Soil and Water Conservation District.
  • Statewide associations of county officers: the Florida Sheriffs Association's sheriff directory and the Florida Clerks of Court Operations Corporation's list name the sheriff and clerk of all 67 counties. County commissioners outside Broward and Miami-Dade come from the Florida Association of Counties directory, which also keeps some former members, so a county is published only when its list matches a real board size (five or seven commissioners). The rest are held back and listed under "Not yet covered". Association lists can trail an election by weeks or months.
  • County Supervisors of Elections: where a county's elections office lists its office holders in a page that can be read without JavaScript (so far Escambia, Franklin, Gadsden, Lee, Leon and Seminole), it is preferred over the statewide association lists for the sheriff, clerk, property appraiser, tax collector, superintendent and commission. When two sources name different people for a one-person office, the county's own elections office wins.
  • School district websites: the board-members page of each district the Florida School Boards Association links to, where the page can be read without running JavaScript.
  • County GIS: Broward County's "Electoral District Boundaries" layer names who holds each County Commission, School Board and city commission district seat. It also supplies the mayors of the few Broward cities whose websites refuse automated requests; those were checked against each city's own page when they were added.
  • City and town websites: each city's own "Mayor and Commission" or "Town Council" page, read by a collector that recognises the ways these pages list their members ("Mayor Jane Doe", "Jane Doe, Vice Mayor", a name above a title). If a page suddenly lists far more or far fewer people than the body has seats, nothing is changed and the page is flagged for a person to check.
  • Special district websites: board pages of the South Broward Drainage District, the Children's Services Council of Broward County and the South Florida Water Management District. Water control, drainage and improvement districts in Broward come from Broward County GIS, and Miami-Dade's community development districts, with the ordinance that created each, from Miami-Dade County GIS.

Only information officials publish in their public role is collected: name, office, district, party, and office contact details.

Portraits are linked, not copied. For members of Congress they come from a public-domain image set built from the official Congressional Biographical Directory, matched to each member by state, district and surname. For other officials the portrait comes from the official's own government page or county GIS records. Where no official photo could be found, a freely licensed photo from Wikimedia Commons (the image library behind Wikipedia) may be used; its author and licence are credited on the profile. Birth dates missing from official sources may likewise come from Wikidata, and profiles link to a Wikipedia article when one exists. These public, community-edited sources are used only to fill gaps, never to replace official information, and may occasionally be out of date. When there is no photo, or it fails to load, the person's initials are shown instead.

District maps come from the U.S. Census Bureau (Congress, the Florida Legislature, counties and cities), Broward County GIS (County Commission, School Board, city commission and water control districts and the legal city limits), Miami-Dade County GIS (County Commission, School Board and community development districts) and City of Miami GIS (its commission districts). On the Your representatives page, what you type in the address box is sent to Esri's ArcGIS World Geocoder to suggest and place addresses, and to the Census Bureau's address finder if Esri can't find it. Neither is told who you are, and this site stores nothing you type.

How information is collected

An automated collector visits each source page, reads the published roster, and records what it finds. It identifies itself honestly to each website, follows each site's robots.txt rules, waits between requests, and never tries to get past logins or access restrictions.

Every record keeps a link to the page it came from, so you can always check the original.

How often records are refreshed

Every source is collected again once a day. Each profile shows the date its record was last confirmed against its source, and the data sources page shows the status of every source.

How accuracy is verified

  • Names, phone numbers, email addresses and web addresses are checked before they are stored. Values that fail the checks are dropped, not repaired.
  • Changes found by the collector are not published automatically. Each change, including officials who no longer appear on a source, is reviewed by a person first.
  • If a source fails, or suddenly lists far fewer people than before, the previous records are kept rather than replaced.
  • Likely duplicate records are flagged for review.
  • An "Elected office" label describes how the office is normally filled. An individual may have been appointed to finish a term after a vacancy.

How missing information is handled

If a source does not publish a detail, the field is left empty and labelled "Not published by the source". Nothing is guessed or inferred. The only exception is the gap-filling from Wikimedia Commons and Wikidata described above, which is always credited.

What is and is not included

Report incorrect information

If you find an error, contact the operator of this site with a link to the profile and, if you can, a link to an official page showing the correct information.

Corrections are checked against the official source before a record is changed. Because the directory mirrors official websites, the fastest fix is often for the office itself to update its own page; the change is then picked up at the next collection.
